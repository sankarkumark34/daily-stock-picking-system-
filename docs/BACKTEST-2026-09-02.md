## Why it loses — diagnosis from the numbers

1. **Stop is hit 1.6× more often than the target.** With a 1.6 : 1 reward/risk the break-even target-hit rate is ≈ 38 %; the model achieves 25 %. Short-term (5–7 day) continuation of already-extended momentum names mean-reverts more often than it continues.
2. **Expired trades are mildly positive** (≈ +1 % gross on average in the smoke runs) — i.e. the *direction* is weakly right over the hold, but the ATR stop converts normal volatility into realised losses.
3. **Selection is monotone in trend/momentum**, so it buys the most extended stocks every day; sector, regime and volume factors only reorder within that set.
4. Costs (0.39 %) matter but are not the cause: gross expectancy is still ≈ −0.02 % per pick.

## What this does and does not say

- It **does** say: this specific rule set (momentum/trend long entries, ATR stop, ~2×ATR target, 5–7 day hold, top-10 by composite score) has no repeatable edge on NSE 2020–2026 after costs.
- It **does not** say that no daily-picks system can work. Candidate research directions (each needs its own walk-forward test, not parameter fiddling on this one):
  - exit design: no hard ATR stop / wider stop with time exit, or trailing exits;
  - horizon: 20-day holds for trend setups (momentum is a 1–12 month effect, not 5 days);
  - mean-reversion setups (pullback / reversal) tested on their own instead of competing with momentum in one score;
  - regime-conditional strategy switching;
  - an ML probability model (P[target before stop]) — only meaningful once a setup family shows a base rate above break-even.

## Caveats

- Sector mapping uses the current Nifty 500 list (survivorship bias in the *sector strength* factor only; the stock universe itself is point-in-time).
- Fundamentals are neutral (no point-in-time source); F&O/OI data not yet wired in.
- Fills assume next-day open with 0.05 % slippage; ambiguous bars (target and stop touched the same day) are counted as losses.
- Delivery % coverage is partial for some historical dates.
